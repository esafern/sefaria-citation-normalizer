# Yad Malachi — Klalei HaGemara, **Gimel section** — same passage, every scan's OCR

Raw OCR of the Gimel section (its two opening lemmas), from the embedded text layer of **every scan in hand**.

**Direction & fidelity:** Google Books stores its text layer in *visual* order, so each line is mechanically reversed to logical Hebrew here; HebrewBooks stores *logical* order and is used as-is. No OCR/spelling correction is applied — this is the untouched embedded layer. Numbers and abbreviations can sit slightly off after reversal.

**What to notice:** every block below is the *same passage*, but the raw embedded OCR varies enormously. **Berlin** (square) OCRs cleanly and completely. The **Przemyśl** Google scans, though also square, OCR with heavy letter-confusion (e.g. `מדברי רש"י` comes out `מדנלי לס"י`) — square type is necessary but not sufficient; scan quality matters. The *second* scan of Przemyśl 1877 (**HebrewBooks #14122**) has better letter accuracy but is badly segmented (≈one word per line) — a **different error profile on the same edition**, which is exactly what makes a second scan worth voting in. The **Rashi**-script Livorno first edition is essentially unreadable to this OCR — which is why the plan runs a Rashi-specific engine (Jochre) on it and builds the base text from the square editions. In short: this is the raw disagreement an ensemble exists to resolve.
